# Introduction

This is one of the apps from my suite of Language apps; [nalelu](https://github.com/nel-sam/nalelu) (Japanese), [HanDrills](https://github.com/nel-sam/handrills) (Korean), and [Navika](https://github.com/nel-sam/navika) (Serbian/Croatian). This app drills specifically in Japanese and currently supports drilling counters, kanji, and verb conjugations.

## History and why I built the language apps

The projects started about 6 years but after starting at my last company I essentially abandoned my apps for about 4 years, and have just recently restarted developing them.

I originally created the apps because I love learning languages but found that existing apps tried to hard to make it fun and easy to learn languages, but rarely produces results.

From my experience, especially at the beginning, language learning kind of has to be repetitive and sometimes a little boring. For me, the biggest leaps in progress in language learning were after an hour or so of drilling very specific grammar points or vocab. The repetition makes the concepts second nature, and that was the original goal of the 3 apps. As I started become more interested in AI integration I thought of ways in which AI could improve the user experience in my apps without getting in the way. I have a few ideas in mind that I will be implementing over time but first my goal is to learn the tech, this I've created the Coach feature as a POC of the tech and as a way to get the foundation laid for what will become a much improved UI/UX on my apps.

## The future of the projects

Now that I'm back at developing the suite of apps, I have some goals for the projects

1. Improved and modernized UI/UX. While I worked with a designer for the menus and navigation, the exercises themselves I created back when my sense for aesthetics and UI/UX were still unrefined. Improving the UI/UX of the drill exercises is an area I plan to focus on.
2. Tasteful additions of AI features. The Coach feature currently in progress is part POC part possible useful feature, but in the future I see AI serving as a tool to help explain the grammar you drill in the exercises. Nothing revolutionary, nothing world-changing, but definitely something I would have appreciated when first learning the languages the apps teach.
3. Mini-games. When I was first learning Japanese I found some online mini games that helped me learn the alphabets and vocab. I see my apps incorporating games where it makes sense.
